We move it at every tier

Automotive supply chains have tiers. Tier 1 suppliers move complete sub-assemblies. Tier 2 move components. Tier 3 are material suppliers. Each tier has a different rhythm, a different volume, a different destination.

T1

Tier 1

Complete sub-assemblies from specialized manufacturers direct to OEM plants.

T2

Tier 2

Component parts from secondary suppliers consolidated and moved to Tier 1.

T3

Tier 3

Raw materials and basic inputs moved to component manufacturers.

AFM

Aftermarket

Replacement parts and service components to dealers and repair shops.

SR

Spare & Related

OEM spare parts and accessories distributed to dealers globally.

What we handle, and why each one is awkward

Automotive components range from soft castings to volatile batteries. Each category has different handling requirements, storage conditions, and regulatory compliance.

Powertrain castings & forgings

+

Heavy, fragile, and prone to corrosion if not handled properly. Requires climate-controlled storage and damage prevention measures.

Bulk plastic & glass

+

High-volume, low-value components that break easily. Efficient consolidation and careful stacking prevents damage and waste.

Electronics & bits

+

Small, high-value items vulnerable to theft and static damage. Requires secure facilities and ESD protection measures.

Lithium batteries & panels

+

Hazardous materials with strict transportation regulations. Requires certified handling, special packaging, and expert documentation.

Steel, lots & fasteners

+

Bulk tonnage requiring heavy-duty storage and load-bearing capacity. Susceptible to moisture and requires rust prevention measures.

Service & aftermarket parts

+

Diverse inventory with wide product range. Requires efficient pick-and-pack, multi-channel distribution, and fast fulfillment.

Batteries are the hardest thing in the vehicle to move

EV batteries are hazardous materials classified for air, sea, road, and rail transport. Every transit method has different packing requirements, documentation, and regulatory approval. OPS manages the complexity of moving batteries from cell manufacturers through final assembly.

The long tail nobody plans for

After a vehicle launches, the supply chain does not end. Parts wear out, recalls are issued, service centers need inventory, and field teams need replacement stock. The long tail of the supply chain lasts longer than the initial build.

Dealer and service center restocking on demand

Warranty claims and recalls requiring rapid distribution

Field teams and mobile units needing micro-fulfillment
