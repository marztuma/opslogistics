Customs & Compliance

A customs entry is a legal declaration.

Not paperwork. It is a statement to a government about what your goods are, what they are worth and where they came from — and you carry the liability for it, whoever filed it. We keep clearance in-house so that liability sits with people we manage.

Four ways customs costs you money

Customs failures create direct costs through penalties and indirect costs through delays. Both hit your margins.

Slow tariff classification

Wrong HS code means wrong duty rate. Getting the code right requires product knowledge and trade rules experience.

Incomplete documentation

Missing certificates, invoices, or origin paperwork trigger holds. A missing document can delay a shipment for weeks.

Slow timing friction

Filing declarations manually or waiting for approvals adds days. Each day adds cost in warehousing and working capital.

Customs good labels

Mislabeled goods create additional inspections. Inspections mean more time, more cost, and increased risk of seizure.

What clearance actually involves

01

Classification

Assigning the correct HS code based on product composition and use.

02

Valuation

Determining the dutiable value of the goods for duty calculation.

03

Origin

Verifying country of origin for duty rate and trade agreement eligibility.

04

Licenses & controls

Checking for restricted items, licenses, and regulatory compliance requirements.

05

Entry & declaration

Filing the customs entry and making declarations on behalf of the importer.

06

Duty & assessment

Calculating and arranging payment of all applicable duties and taxes.

What to have ready before you ship

Speed through customs depends on complete documentation. Missing one piece delays the entire entry.

✓

Commercial invoice

Itemized list with product descriptions, quantities, and prices.

✓

Packing list

Breakdown of contents by box with weights and dimensions.

✓

Bill of lading

Proof of shipment and carrier documentation.

✓

Certificate of origin

Document proving the country where goods were manufactured.

✓

Export/import permits

Any required licenses for controlled or restricted goods.

✓

Technical documents

Specs, certifications, or standards compliance documents if applicable.

The part that saves money rather than time

Duty optimization is about knowing what tools are available and whether your goods qualify. Most importers leave savings on the table by not exploring duty reduction programs.

•

Preferential trade agreements

FTA, GSP, and other preferential rates can reduce duty by 50% or more.

•

Bonded warehousing

Defer duties while goods are in storage, then only pay when goods leave for sale.

•

Drawback claims

Recover duties paid on materials that are exported in finished products.

•

Tariff reclassification

Challenge HS codes through formal processes if duties can be reduced.
