What we were asked to fix, and what happened next.

Each of these logistics cases started with a problem nobody had solved. We redesigned their supply chain, rebuilt their operations, and proved that the cost came down when the service went up. Here are the stories.

Three problems we were brought in on

We started where every logistics engagement starts: understanding what was broken and why the existing supply chain was costing more than it solved.

Client profile image

Problem 1: Inefficiency

What we found: Their logistics network had too many hubs and too much transfer movement. Costs were locked in place by decades of inertia.

Problem 2: Visibility

What we found: Shipment tracking was manual. Nobody actually knew where cargo was until it arrived. Delays were surprises.

Problem 3: Reporting

What we found: They had no dashboard. Insights came from quarterly reviews. By then, the problems were locked in.

Case study 1 image

Before & Background

Their network was built decades ago and never redesigned. Seven distribution centers for a footprint that could use three.

What we changed

Closed four underutilized centers. Consolidated to three strategic locations. Reduced dwell time by 40%.

What happened

Cost dropped 28%. Delivery speed increased 35%. The network was smaller, faster, and more profitable.

The discipline that matters

Network design is not about moving more cargo. It's about moving the same cargo with fewer stops and lower cost.

This client learned that consolidation saves more than growth ever does.

Case study 2 image

Before & Background

Tracking was done by phone calls to carriers. No automated visibility. Surprises were the norm.

What we changed

Built real-time tracking dashboard integrated with carrier APIs. Automated alerts for delays and exceptions.

What happened

Visibility improved to 100%. Customer satisfaction increased 42%. They could now prevent problems instead of reacting to them.

The discipline that matters

Visibility is not a luxury feature. It's the foundation of proactive logistics management.

This client learned that knowing what's happening is more valuable than discovering it later.

Case study 3 image

Before & Background

Reporting was manual and quarterly. No way to identify cost drivers or optimization opportunities in real time.

What we changed

Deployed live analytics dashboard. Weekly cost breakdowns by lane, mode, and vendor. Automated cost alerts.

What happened

Cost tracking shifted from quarterly to weekly. They identified $2.3M in annual savings. Now they manage costs actively instead of discovering them later.

The discipline that matters

Reporting is not about looking back. It's about seeing what's happening now so you can fix it tomorrow.

This client learned that real-time data is the only way to stay ahead of rising costs.
