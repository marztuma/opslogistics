Cold chain cargo fails quietly.

Maintaining cold chain integrity is expensive. The upside is value in the cargo. The downside is the product is a loss if a single temperature excursion ruins it. It is critical to have it and to verify that it stayed cold the entire way.

It's a real problem.
Pharma Biotech Food & beverage Life sciences

Four bands, four different problems

Temperature range determines what product moves where, and what breaks where if the product gets too hot or too cold during shipment.

Ultra-cold

-80°C to -50°C. Vaccines, specific biologics, and high-value therapeutics require extreme cold.

Frozen

-25°C to -15°C. Frozen foods, frozen plasma, and most frozen biologics live in this range.

Chilled

2°C to 8°C. The standard for most pharmaceuticals, blood products, and many fresh foods.

Controlled ambient

15°C to 25°C. Temperature-controlled storage for products that require protection from heat.

The chain breaks at the joints

Maintaining temperature is hardest at transitions—when product moves between equipment, location, or carrier. These moments demand careful handoff and continuous monitoring.

•

Shipper

Initial packing and thermal preparation of shipment.

•

Temperature excursion

Any deviation from target range during transit or storage.

•

Thermal & sensors

Equipment must maintain temperature and record it continuously.

•

Ambient negligence

External temperature impacts equipment ability to maintain cold.

•

Data integrity

Records must be complete, accurate, and uninterrupted.

•

Port delays

Unplanned delays at ports or borders extend exposure time.

Active, passive, or both

Different cargo demands different methods. Passive systems are cheaper. Active systems are more reliable.

Passive container

Insulated box with ice packs or gel. No power required. Best for short shipments.

Passive & units

Combination of insulation and active cooling units. Reliable for longer journeys.

Powered air container

Electrically powered refrigeration. Most reliable but requires power source.

Insulated carriage

Truck or container with built-in insulation and cooling systems.

Dry ice packaging

Dry ice for ultra-cold. High-risk, high-cost, but effective for critical cargo.

Cold storage

Warehouse-based temperature control during storage or dwell time.

Regulatory requirement

If it was not recorded, it did not stay cold

Continuous monitoring and documentation is non-negotiable for regulated shipments. Pharma products require unbroken temperature data from shipper to receiver.

What happens when it goes outside range

Minor deviation

Brief excursion outside range may be acceptable depending on product stability data. Document it. Review with shipper.

Significant deviation

Extended excursion or multiple peaks require stability testing or potential product loss. Risk assessment needed.

Product loss

Cargo is compromised and cannot be released for use. Total financial loss. Investigation into root cause required.
