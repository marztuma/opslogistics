Small, light, and worth more than the truck.

Technology freight breaks the weight-to-value ratio. A small parcel can hold a lot of monetary value and a lot of regulatory care. Shipping tech requires tracking, temperature control, proof of handling, and documentation that proves chains-of-custody.

Let's Talk Technology

Two identical pallets, two completely different problems

The handling requirements depend on what is in the pallet. Standard cargo is weight-focused. Finished devices are liability-focused.

Standard cargo

  • Weight per unit controls cost
  • Handling is mechanical—stack, move, unload
  • Proof of arrival is sufficient
  • Damage is cost of freight insurance

Finished devices

  • Value per unit controls cost
  • Handling is protective—careful movement, climate control
  • Proof of condition and chain of custody required
  • Damage becomes liability and customer dissatisfaction

The stage where nothing is allowed to happen

Technology in transit is a high-consequence logistics problem. It cannot be dropped, cannot get wet, cannot get too hot or too cold.

Departure from facility

Verification of condition, proper packaging, environmental seal, and documentation before handoff to carrier.

Handoff points

Each carrier transition requires inspection and re-sealing. Conditions must be documented at each point.

Dwell locations

Temperature and environmental monitoring at warehouses and transfer hubs. Climate control is non-negotiable.

Arrival

Inspection for damage, seal verification, and documentation of condition before handoff to customer or deployment.

Any break in the chain—a dent, a temperature excursion, a missing seal—becomes a warranty claim and a customer problem.

Four gates, and all four have to pass

Technology logistics has compliance gates at every stage. Failure at any gate means the shipment cannot proceed.

Classification

Determine regulatory classification and handling category.

Where it's going

Destination and carrier capabilities must match requirements.

Packaging right

Protective packaging for transit method must be verified.

Insurance cover

Coverage must match the declared value and risk profile.

All four gates are mandatory. Tech devices cannot ship without passing each one. Miss one and the shipment does not leave the facility.

Most tech ships more than once

Warranty claims, failed components, and device recalls create reverse logistics flows. Some tech ships forward multiple times as units get refurbished and re-deployed.

RMA & warranty returns

Defective units back to factory for evaluation.

Recall logistics

Damaged or safety-critical units recalled from field.

Refurbishment & recovery

Units repaired and re-certified for resale or redeployment.

Cross-loading results

Refurbished units shipped to new customers or regions.

Delivered is not the same as installed

Getting the device to the destination is only half the problem. Installation, configuration, and initial testing determine whether the customer accepts the shipment. A delivered device that does not power on is a failed delivery.

Tech logistics often includes site assessment, power and network setup, initial configuration, and operator training. The warehouse job is only where it starts.
